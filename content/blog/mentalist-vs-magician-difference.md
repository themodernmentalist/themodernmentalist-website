A magician's entertainment is built around objects, cards, coins, props, and sleight of hand designed to create a visual "how did they do that" moment. A mentalist works almost entirely with people: reading reactions, predicting decisions and creating moments that feel personal rather than performed. Both are forms of magic; the difference is where the mystery is aimed.

## What Magic Focuses On

Traditional magic is prop-driven and visual. It's brilliant for spectacle and works well when the goal is a striking, shareable moment, think card tricks at a table, or a larger illusion on stage.

## What Mentalism Focuses On

Mentalism leans on psychology, intuition and suggestion. Instead of "how did the coin disappear," the question becomes "how did he know that." It tends to feel more intimate and more personal, because the performance is built around the specific people in the room rather than a fixed routine.

## Which Suits Your Event?

**Choose magic if:** you want a highly visual spectacle, a strong stage moment, or entertainment aimed more at a "wow" reaction than a conversation starter.

**Choose mentalism if:** you want guests talking to each other afterwards, an experience that feels tailored to your event rather than generic, or entertainment suited to a sophisticated, slightly sceptical audience, corporate leadership teams and wedding guests included.

In practice, many modern performers, including mentalists who also work close-up, blend elements of both: a card is used, but what matters is the thought behind it, not the card itself.

### FAQ

**Is mentalism just magic with a different name?**
Not quite. Magic typically relies on physical methods, mentalism leans on psychological ones, reading people, influencing choices, predicting decisions. The audience experience is different as a result.

**Which is better for a corporate audience?**
Mentalism tends to land particularly well with corporate and executive audiences, since it feels intelligent and interactive rather than purely visual, and needs no stage setup.

**Can a performer do both in one event?**
Yes. It's common to open with lighter, prop-based moments before moving into mentalism as the evening's centrepiece.
