The right format depends less on preference and more on your event's structure: how many guests, how the room is laid out, and where in the evening the entertainment needs to sit. Close-up and stage work solve different problems, and the best events often use both.

## Close-Up: Mentalism at Arm's Length

Close-up work, including a dedicated Magic Booth format, puts the performance directly in front of small groups, at a table, during a drinks reception, or in a quiet corner guests step into on their own terms. It suits:

- Drinks receptions and cocktail hours, where guests are mingling rather than seated
- Smaller private dinners where everyone can be reached individually
- Corporate events where you want continuous engagement rather than a single scheduled moment

The advantage is intimacy: reactions build gradually as word spreads through the room, and guests often return more than once to see it again.

## Stage: A Shared Moment for the Whole Room

A stage or private show format brings the whole room's attention to one place at one time. It suits:

- Larger guest counts where close-up work can't reach everyone
- A scheduled highlight moment, after dinner, before a first dance, or as a conference closer
- Events where you want a single, unifying "everyone saw this together" moment

## Combining Both

Many events use close-up during the reception to build energy, then move into a shorter stage set later on as the evening's centrepiece. It's a natural progression: guests arrive curious, then come together for a shared payoff later.

### FAQ

**How many guests can close-up mentalism reach?**
It scales with time rather than a hard guest limit, more time in the room means more guests reached, which is why formats like the Magic Booth are often run across a whole reception rather than a fixed slot.

**Is a stage show better for a corporate conference?**
Generally yes, since it guarantees the full audience experiences the same moment at the same time, which matters for a keynote-adjacent slot.

**Can the same performer do both in one event?**
Yes, this is one of the more common bookings: close-up during drinks, followed by a timed stage or private show later in the schedule.
