Hiring a magician in London comes down to four decisions: which style of performer suits your guests, which format suits your room, how early you book, and whether the person you speak to is the person who turns up. Get those right and the entertainment becomes the part of the night people are still talking about the next day.

## Start With the Style of Performer

"Magician" covers a wide range of acts, and the differences matter more than most people expect.

- **Close-up magicians** work at arm's length with small groups, ideal for drinks receptions, dinners and mingling
- **Stage magicians** perform for a seated room, usually as a scheduled highlight
- **Mentalists** build the performance around guests' thoughts and decisions rather than props, which tends to feel more personal and works well with sophisticated or sceptical audiences
- **Hosts and MCs with magic** hold the evening together while weaving moments of mystery into the programme

If you're unsure which suits you, the difference between a mentalist and a magician is worth reading before you shortlist anyone.

## Match the Format to Your Room

Think about how your guests will actually spend the evening before you think about the act.

**A standing drinks reception** suits close-up work, because guests move between small groups and the performance meets them where they are. **A seated dinner** can take either, with close-up at the tables during courses or a short show once everyone is settled. **A larger conference or party** often works best with a stage set as the shared highlight, plus close-up either side of it.

The strongest events frequently combine formats, close-up early to build the buzz, then a short stage moment that gives the whole room something to share.

## What to Look For When You Shortlist

- **Footage of real audiences.** Showreels filmed in rooms like yours, with unscripted reactions, tell you more than a polished promo
- **Experience at your type of event.** Corporate, private and wedding audiences each need a different touch
- **Recent, verifiable testimonials.** Named clients and specific details are a better sign than generic praise
- **Clear communication from the first reply.** The way a performer handles your enquiry is usually how they'll handle your event

## Questions Worth Asking Before You Book

1. **Who exactly will perform?** Some agencies sell one name and send another
2. **How long is the performance, and where does it sit in the evening?** Timing shapes the whole programme
3. **What do you need from the venue?** Space, sound and sightlines are rarely complicated, but they should be agreed in advance
4. **Is the act tailored to my event?** The best performers shape the material around your guests rather than running a standard set
5. **What are the booking terms?** Deposit, contract and cancellation arrangements should be clear in writing

## When to Book

Peak dates fill early: weekends in spring and summer for weddings and private celebrations, and November and December for corporate parties. Enquiring as soon as your date is confirmed protects your first choice. Last-minute bookings can still work, but you'll have fewer performers to choose from.

## Understand How Pricing Works

Fees for experienced performers are quoted per event, based on the format, duration, date, guest count and location, rather than taken from a fixed price list. A short conversation about your event is the quickest route to an accurate proposal. Our guide to what it costs to hire a mentalist in London breaks the factors down in more detail.

## Make the Booking Straightforward

A good booking process is simple: you share your date, venue and the shape of the event, receive a tailored proposal, confirm with a deposit and contract, then complete a short planning questionnaire so nothing is left to chance on the night. If any part of that feels vague, ask before you commit.

### FAQs

**How far in advance should I hire a magician in London?**
As soon as your date is confirmed. Peak dates, particularly spring and summer weekends and the run-up to Christmas, book well ahead, though shorter-notice enquiries are always worth making.

**Should I choose a magician or a mentalist?**
A magician's act typically centres on props and visual effects. A mentalist's centres on people, their thoughts and decisions, which often feels more personal and suits audiences who prefer something sharper and less theatrical.

**Can one performer cover both close-up and stage?**
Yes. Many events work best with both: close-up during the reception to build momentum, and a short stage set as a shared highlight later in the evening.

**What should I prepare for the performer?**
Usually just the timings, the room layout and a sense of your guests. A good performer will guide you through anything else.
